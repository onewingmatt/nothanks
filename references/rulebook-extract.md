## Components

- 33 cards numbered **3 – 35**
- 55 pass tokens ("chips")
- Rules sheet

## Overview

> In this game you want to get the fewest points possible. Each card is worth its face value. You start with a hand of Pass tokens. These Pass tokens subtract from your total score, but you need them to avoid taking high scoring cards that you don't want. You start your turn with one available face up card. If you take the card (and any Pass tokens on the card), it goes in front of you and another card replaces it. If you don't want a card, simply put a token on the card and then play passes to your left. If you do take cards, you ultimately want them in sequential runs. Individual runs are scored only by the number of the lowest valued card in the run.

## Setup

1. Before starting, determine the number of rounds to play — **the rulebook recommends four rounds**, keeping a cumulative score (pen and paper). You may also play a single scoring round.
2. Shuffle the cards. **Count out 9 cards and set them aside** for this round, do not look at them. The remaining **24 cards** form a face-down draw deck.
3. Flip over the top card and place it next to the draw deck, visible to all — **the active card**.
4. **Each player takes 11 pass tokens** (6 players: 9 tokens each; 7 players: 7 tokens each). Return remaining tokens to the box.
5. **Keep your tokens secret in your hand.** [cross-check: "During the game, all players should keep their chips hidden in their hand so that the other players cannot see them" — Z-Man EN ed.]
6. If you most recently received a gift, go first. [cross-check: randomly determine the starting player — Z-Man EN ed.]

## Gameplay (on your turn)

Either:

1. **Place a Pass token on the active card** — the turn passes to your left. **You may not pass if you have no Pass tokens.**
2. **Take the active card** and add it to your play area. In addition, take the Pass tokens on it and add them to your (hidden) supply. You then **turn over a new active card** from the draw deck and **continue your turn** making the same decision again.

## Play area

Cards you take are face-up, public. Overlap cards to show sequences.

## End of round

The round ends when you take the **last active card** and there are **no cards left in the draw deck** to replace it.

## Scoring (worked examples from the rulebook)

1. "Each card in your play area is worth its face value in points. But it is worth nothing if a card is part of a sequential run and it isn't the lowest card in that sequence."
2. **Example — Brad:** cards {8, 13, 14, 15, 17}. The 8 is isolated → 8 points. The 13, 14, 15 are a run → 13 points (lowest). The 17 is isolated → 17 points. **Card point total = 38 (8+13+17).** If he had the 16 as well, the 17 would connect: total 21 (8+13).
3. "After you have tallied your card points, subtract 1 point for each Pass token you have left in your hand." **Brad had 38 card points and 13 pass tokens → total 25 (38−13).**

Additional worked examples from the Z-Man EN edition transcription (UltraBoardGames / 64 Ounce):

- **Sabrina:** four single cards {4, 6, 10, 21} → 41 points.
- **Peter:** 13 (single) + 15 (run 15–16? per text "13 for the single card, and 15 for the sequence"; sequence 15–16 counts as 15) → 28 points; if he gets the 14, run 13–16 counts as 13.
- **Lisa (end-of-game):** three single cards and two sequences → card points 59 (3+7+10+14+25); 8 chips left → **final score 51 (59−8)**.

## Variants (rules sheet)

- **Hidden Tiger (by Larry Chong):** from the 9 set-aside cards, deal one face-down to each player (lookable); any turn, play it into your play area and continue your turn; if never played it doesn't count.
- **Amigo Variant (3–5 players):** 10 pass tokens each; remove cards 10, 20, and 30 from play (or 10, 19, 28); shuffle and remove six more random cards; knowing three of the nine gaps in advance increases tactics.
